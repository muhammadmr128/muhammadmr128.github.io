Culvert Replacement Job Package

Sizing and planning the replacement of an undersized culvert on a mine access road near Labrador City, using real Wabush Lake rainfall data and Newfoundland and Labrador design rules, from hydraulics to estimate, schedule, bids and inspection.

Independent practice exercise using public documents. Not an IOC or Rio Tinto design, and it uses no company data. Assumptions are clearly marked.

The scenario

A rusted 600 mm corrugated steel culvert under a gravel mine access road (pickups and service trucks, not haul trucks) overtops every spring. This is a hypothetical site, modelled on the kind of work IOC holds a 2025 provincial culvert permit for on its Hillside Access Road.

The package plans the replacement the way a small sustaining-capital job runs: size it, estimate it, schedule it, manage the risks, choose a contractor and inspect the work.

How the job comes together

Step 1

Design flood from Wabush Lake rainfall

Assumed catchment: 40 ha of forest and bog, 1,500 m longest flow path, 1% slope.

Time of concentration (how long rain takes to reach the pipe from the farthest point), Kirpich formula:

tc = 0.0195 × L0.77 × S−0.385 = 0.0195 × 15000.77 × 0.01−0.385 ≈ 32 minutesSo the 30-minute storm is the one to use.

Rational method, with C = 0.4 (Q in m³/s, i in mm/h, A in hectares):

Q = C × i × A ÷ 360

  • Worked: 0.4 × 40.8 × 40 ÷ 360 = 1.81 m³/s

Takeawayclimate change adds about 27% to the 10-year flood, and using only historical rainfall would undersize the pipe. I used 2041 to 2070 values because a pipe installed now will still be in service then.

Step 2

Pipe size, checked against the NL culvert guideline

The guideline's two tests for culverts under 2 m: (1) pass the 10-year flood with no head at the inlet (water no higher than the pipe), even with 0.3 m of gravel allowed in the bottom of pipes over 0.75 m; (2) pass the 100-year flood using the head available at the inlet. I used full-flow capacity with no head, which is conservative.

Manning's equation, with n = 0.024 (corrugated steel) and S = 1%:

Q = (1/n) × A × R2/3 × S1/2

  • 1,400 mm full: A = 1.539 m², R = 0.35 m → Q = 41.67 × 1.539 × 0.497 × 0.1 ≈ 3.19 m³/s
  • With 0.3 m gravel: A ≈ 1.30 m², wetted perimeter ≈ 4.20 m, R ≈ 0.31 m → Q ≈ 2.47 m³/s

Other NL rules built in: set the pipe 0.3 m into the stream bed so natural bed material lines it (the guideline asks for no more than 0.9 m/s at normal flow); riprap at both ends with the toe below the bed; remove shipping braces that could trap ice; inspect in spring for ice and debris.

Takeawaythe 1,400 mm pipe is the smallest size that passes both tests once the gravel deposit is counted.

Step 3

Quantities and estimate

  • Trench depth: 1.2 m cover + 1.4 m pipe + 0.15 m bedding = 2.75 m. At 1.2 m or deeper, NL safety rules require shoring or sloping, so the sides are 1H:1V.
  • Bottom width 1.4 + 2 × 0.5 = 2.4 m; top width 2.4 + 2 × 2.75 = 7.9 m; pipe length 22 m
  • Excavation (2.4 + 7.9) ÷ 2 × 2.75 × 22 ≈ 312 m³; bedding ≈ 8 m³; granular pipe zone ≈ 56 m³; native backfill ≈ 214 m³

Takeawayat a remote site almost half the cost is fixed, so bundling several culverts into one mobilization cuts the cost per crossing.

Step 4

Schedule and permits

Weeks from the start of June.

NL guidelines prefer installing crossings in summer low flow. Fisheries and Oceans Canada (DFO) bars in-water work on tributaries and headwaters of scheduled salmon rivers in Labrador from September 15 to June 15. Field work sits after the freshet and before freeze-up.

The Section 48 permit is issued under the Water Resources Act.

Takeawaythe schedule works backwards from the in-water window, so permits and pipe lead time decide the start date.

Step 5

Main risks

Rating is likelihood × consequence.

RiskRatingMitigation
Sediment release to the streamHigh (3 × 5)Silt fence and check dams first, divert flow and work in the dry, turbidity checks downstream
Late pipe delivery to a remote siteMedium (3 × 4)Order at award, written lead time, two weeks of float
Rain or high flow during excavationMedium (3 × 4)August low flow, bypass pump on standby, stop-work trigger
Trench collapse (2.75 m deep)Medium (2 × 5)Slope 1H:1V or trench box; competent person inspects each shift
Conflict with mine trafficMedium (3 × 3)Detour agreed with operations, flaggers, set closure times

Takeawaysediment is the one high risk, so the controls go in before the first bucket of excavation.

Step 6

Choosing a contractor

Weights: price 40%, safety 25%, schedule 15%, experience 10%, method and environmental controls 10%.

Price score = lowest price ÷ this price × 100

  • Three fictional bids: A $121,000, B $104,000, C $129,000
  • Weighted scores: C 87.2 (recommended), A 84.5, B 77.1

Takeawaythe cheapest bid (B) is 9% below the estimate and ranks last once safety and schedule count. A bid far below the estimate is a signal to check what it leaves out.

Step 7

Inspection and test plan

Nine checks. At the four hold points, work stops until the check is signed off.

  1. Pre-start: permit and DFO advice on site, utilities located, sediment controls and bypass in placeHold point
  2. Excavation: width, depth, side slope, ladder
  3. Subgrade: firm and unfrozen; soft spots replacedHold point
  4. Bedding: 150 mm, 1% grade, invert 0.3 m below the stream bed
  5. Pipe install: 1,400 mm CSP, inverts within 25 mm, shipping braces removedHold point
  6. Backfill: lifts no thicker than 200 mm, at least 95% Standard Proctor, tested each lift
  7. Riprap: stone size, toe below bed, slopes no steeper than 1H:1V
  8. Road restoration: 150 mm surface gravel, matching crossfall
  9. Close-out: as-built survey, photos, controls removed, no ponding at the inletHold point

Takeawaythe hold points sit where a mistake gets buried: under the pipe, around it and at hand-over.

Real data vs my assumptions

Sourced

Real and cited

  • Wabush Lake rainfall (historical and 2041 to 2070)
  • NL culvert design tests and the 0.3 m deposit rule
  • The summer low-flow preference
  • DFO Labrador timing window
  • The NL trench rule at 1.2 m
  • IOC's 2025 Hillside Access Road culvert permit (ALT14415-2025, Section 48, Water Resources Act)
Assumed

My assumptions

  • 40 ha catchment
  • Runoff coefficient 0.4
  • Flow path and slopes
  • Site dimensions
  • Unit rates
  • The three bids

Limits

Rational method on an assumed catchment; Manning capacity instead of a full inlet-control headwater analysis (FHWA HDS-5); no outlet scour sizing or fish passage assessment; illustrative unit rates. A real job would add a site survey, a geotechnical check, the owner's design standards and estimating data, the Section 48 permit and a DFO review.
